# Project Sun: Existing Application Refinement, at a glance

**Source:** Plan 1, `docs/plans/01-existing-app-refinement/plan.md` (version 1.0, 2026-11-10). This summary adds nothing to the plan; the italic references under each area (for example PR-01) point to the full item in that file.

> **What Project Sun does.** It measures how often AI assistants such as ChatGPT name a business when people ask for recommendations. It reads the business's website, agrees its topics and competitors with an operator, asks a fixed set of 30 questions, collects the answers and reports results as "named in x of n answers".
>
> **What this plan is.** Refinements to the existing application only: no new product stage and no recommendations layer. It is a plan, not built work. Every phase needs the product owner's go-ahead, and any use of collection credits or paid model calls needs separate approval.
>
> **Three kinds of question.** *Discovery* questions ask for recommendations without naming the business. *Trade-off* questions compare the business with a named competitor. *Sentiment* questions ask about the business by name.

## The refinements

### Question set (the 30 questions)

| Area / Feature | Current problem / gap | Proposed solution | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business priorities and strengths**<br>*PR-01, PR-16* | Questions come from model-suggested customer needs. The operator never states why the business wins, what it is not for, or which topics matter most. | Capture in the confirmed profile: category in buyers' words, audience, 1–3 reasons the business wins, 1–3 things it is not for, and a priority (P1–P3) per topic; question allocation follows priority. When Search Console is connected, show which needs appear in real searches, as hints only (never volumes). | Priority topics get enough questions; no Discovery question targets what the business does not offer; priorities take under a minute to set. |
| **Labels on every question**<br>*PR-02, PR-05* | Key facts about each question (type, topic, intent, place, whether it names the brand) sit in free text, so results cannot be split by them. Citation figures mix brand-named questions, which over-draw the business's own pages, with neutral ones. | One structured label record per question, frozen at approval (type, topic, intent, buying stage, shape, place level, branded or not, rival, criterion, theme). Every metric states which question types it covers; "known when named" and "found when not named" are shown side by side. | Results can be sliced by topic, intent and place; brand-named questions never inflate neutral Discovery figures. |
| **Coverage and question mix**<br>*PR-03, PR-04, MS-07, PR-09* | Reviewers cannot see whether priority topics, question shapes and places are covered. With 10 Discovery questions (about 2 per topic, one answer each), results carry ±25–30-point uncertainty. | A coverage matrix per question set with minimums (each top-priority topic gets at least 3 Discovery questions in 2 shapes). An owner decision on the Discovery count: the plan leans to 20 Discovery, 10 Trade-off and 10 Sentiment (40 questions). Allow need-based "long-tail" Discovery questions only under strict neutrality checks. | Thin topics are visible without opening the questions; Discovery figures become tighter if the count is raised. |
| **Place wording**<br>*PR-06, GEO-03* | How a question names the place changes results sharply: Atelier 10 was named in 9 of 10 district-worded questions but 3 of 14 city-wide ones. City-less questions sent ChatGPT searching other emirates. Operators never state where they compete. | The operator confirms the competing level (district, city or country). A declared place mix is reported per level, never blended. Every by-name question includes the city. Reports state the scope: ChatGPT on the web, signed out, asked from a given country, in English. | Local results are comparable and honest about place; no misleading city-wide claims. |
| **Question quality before approval**<br>*PR-07, PR-18, PR-19, PR-20* | Ambiguous words caused misreads ("Studio14" searched as a salon; "RBAC" treated as a rival). Quality scores do not reflect quality (malformed questions once scored 90+). The review screen shows raw internal keys, and known validation holes let invalid questions through. | An automatic pre-approval check for acronyms, ambiguous category words and name collisions. A golden-set evaluation (scored checks plus blind human review) run before and after every change. A plain-language review screen with the coverage matrix and an optional small test ask. Close each known validation hole with a regression test. | Fewer weak questions reach approval; every change is measured on the same benchmark; any question's purpose is explainable without internal codes. |
| **Checking questions after answers arrive**<br>*PR-08* | Questions are judged only before answers exist. Questions that repeatedly get no business named, or only a clarifying reply, are never flagged. | A per-question audit across runs (no business named, clarification only, one business only, undecided comparisons, opinion answers listing rivals), flagged for review. | Weak questions are identified from real answers; no headline figure changes without a policy update. |
| **Comparison and opinion questions**<br>*PR-10, PR-11, PR-14* | It is unmeasured whether short "X vs Y" questions leave answers undecided, and uneven competitor lists leave some rivals with few questions. Opinion results are one blend although each question has a theme. Template audience labels read artificial. | First measure from stored answers which wording gets decisive answers; require explicit "which would you pick" questions if they decide more often. At least 2 questions per top rival. Group opinions by topic and theme with counts. Drop template audience labels but keep natural situations ("for my mum"); persona checks only for B2B, later. | More decisive comparisons; opinion findings by theme ("2 of 3 price answers mixed"). |
| **Answer variation and noise**<br>*PR-12, MS-02, MS-03* | Each question gets one answer per run. Identical runs minutes apart flipped 1–3 of 10 outcomes, wording alone moved results, and the noise level has never been measured. | Measure the noise floor: re-collect six benchmark projects on another day (about 300 answers, with approval). Freeze 2 alternative wordings per question for stability checks. Optionally collect 2–3 answers per Discovery question per run. | A published flip rate; "within normal variation" becomes a measured statement. |
| **Refreshing questions without losing history**<br>*PR-17* | Any new question-set version breaks every trend, so fixing one weak question resets history. The questions page can show a stale set after re-confirmation. | A stable key per question carried across versions when wording is unchanged. Measure compares the matched part ("28 of 30 unchanged") with a clear caution. Defined refresh triggers, including a quarterly review. | Small fixes no longer wipe trends; a stale set is visible as soon as the page loads. |
| **Diagnostic checks outside the 30**<br>*PR-13, PR-15* | Today's data cannot tell whether AI misunderstands the business or simply does not trust it, cannot detect wrong facts, and cannot see how the category is framed. Everything is in English only. | Separate, operator-started check runs that never enter headline figures: accuracy questions built from confirmed facts, yes/no understanding questions, and later category framing. Arabic and Hindi as separate baselines, piloted later. | Wrong facts and understanding gaps become detectable without distorting headline numbers. |

### Measure: reliability and reporting

| Area / Feature | Current problem / gap | Proposed solution | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Results on current rules**<br>*MS-01, PL-04* | No stored answer has been analyzed with the latest rules, so reports still show retired findings and a reputation concern that does not exist. 14 legacy runs remain open. | With the owner's OK, re-analyze every completed run ("Update results") and show analysis versions on reports and exports. Close legacy runs with "Finish with what's collected"; never delete them. | Every report reflects current rules and agrees with an independent hand-check. |
| **Claims about change over time**<br>*MS-05, MS-06, MS-08* | Model swaps, provider switches, country changes and missing fields leave no trace, so small movements can be read as real change. | A dated change log per project (automatic and manual events), shown beside comparisons. Call a change "moved" only when statistically significant; otherwise "within normal variation". Later, per-question consistency across 3 or more comparable runs. | Fewer false "improved" or "declined" claims; every comparison shows what else changed. |
| **Run cadence and budget**<br>*MS-04, PL-10* | Runs start only by hand, so nothing repeats, and there is no answer budget or credit view. | Per-project cadence, provider order and monthly answer cap. The console lists due runs with their cost and a "Start due runs" action, plus a budget and cost ledger. Unattended scheduling waits for the robustness work. | Regular, budgeted runs with visible cost. |
| **New Measure readings**<br>*MS-09, MS-10, MS-11, MS-12* | Several useful readings are not shown: when the business's own page is cited but another business is named; verdicts by deciding criterion; sponsored cards beside answers; businesses ChatGPT names repeatedly that are not tracked. | Show "own page cited × business named" with the answers behind each cell. Group verdicts by criterion and opinions by theme (counts only). A sponsored-placement line kept out of organic figures. Surface untracked businesses named in 2 or more answers, with a "track this" option. | Clearer reasons behind absence; paid presence visible but separate; missing competitors surface (e.g. two studios named 3 times each for Studio14). |

### Analysis accuracy and data quality

| Area / Feature | Current problem / gap | Proposed solution | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading answers correctly**<br>*DQ-03 to DQ-07, DQ-09* | The rule-based reader still misreads answers: "no complaints found" counted as negative; hedged recommendations dropped; rankings confused; a product feature ("RBAC") counted as a rival; place and style names ("Fade") counted as businesses; the business's own name variants not recognized. | Fix the opinion, ranking, comparison and name-variant rules as one bundle. Count a name as a business only if it is tracked, confirmed or matched to a listing. Blind human labeling of stored answers measures accuracy and gates the fixes. | Measured accuracy per field; false readings disappear (e.g. Postman's "win" against "RBAC"); no place name leads results. |
| **Own and competitor websites**<br>*DQ-01, DQ-02, DQ-14* | A likely second website of Atelier 10 counts as an independent source, shifting "your site cited" by 5–14 of 30 answers. Wrong competitor websites were frozen (a parked page; a California studio). One business has 12 projects. | Evidence-backed ownership claims: a person confirms, plus one recorded corroboration; pending claims show only "if yours". Never freeze a failed competitor-site check, and require the place or category to match. One primary project per business. | Source figures credit the right owners, and every confirmed change is listed in the report. |
| **Sources and unused stored data**<br>*DQ-08, DQ-10, DQ-12, DQ-13* | Map links are counted as sources. Useful data is collected but never read: ChatGPT's own search queries, sponsored cards, local listings with ratings and reviews, citation titles, follow-up questions. Search state and model are reported misleadingly. | Show map links separately, outside source figures; keep market-specific source tables (e.g. Fresha, Justdial). A versioned reader for stored data, with a warning when a provider stops sending a field. Report search state and model honestly ("model not recorded"). | Cleaner source lists; richer evidence without collecting new answers; early warning when data disappears. |
| **Website reading gaps**<br>*DQ-11* | Some service pages were stored empty although ChatGPT quoted prices from them, so "not stated on the site" warnings can be false. | Retry empty pages (trailing slash, browser fallback); say "could not read" when a page failed; keep outbound links as ownership evidence. | Pages such as Atelier 10's hair page are read correctly; no false "missing information" warnings. |

### Geography and language

| Area / Feature | Current problem / gap | Proposed solution | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where answers are collected from**<br>*GEO-01, GEO-02, GEO-04, GEO-05* | Projects without a market hint are observed from the US: a Bengaluru restaurant's 0 of 10 describes a US user. Some providers cannot target a country, and why a local business is absent is not broken down. | Collect from the business's own market (intake hint, else the profile's country). When most answers come from elsewhere, withhold place findings with a banner, and re-collect the affected project. For local businesses, skip providers that cannot honor the country. Later, split local absence into not retrieved, retrieved but not named, and cited but not named. | Local results describe local customers. |
| **Languages and Google surfaces**<br>*GEO-06, GEO-07, PL-11* | The crawler drops or mishandles non-Latin text. Gemini is built but hidden. The best-documented local lever (Google Business Profile) acts on Google surfaces that are not measured. | Fix non-Latin text handling now; Arabic or Hindi sets later, as new baselines. Decide on Gemini or a Google AI surface before selling local visibility, with clear criteria to re-enable Gemini (budget, same-day check, country honored). | Ready for non-English markets; no unsupported local-visibility claims. |

### Platform and reliability

| Area / Feature | Current problem / gap | Proposed solution | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Robustness and safety**<br>*PL-03, PL-05 to PL-08* | A restart leaves crawls, competitor searches and question sets stuck. Some web fetches bypass the safety guard, malformed requests return server errors, collection status lives only in memory, and some upstream checks are missing (e.g. a failed profile can be confirmed). | Recover interrupted jobs at start-up with a retry. Route every fetch through the safety guard. Return clear request errors. Persist provider health and progress, and prevent duplicate collection runners. Block confirming failed profiles, plus related upstream fixes. | Reliable enough for scheduled runs; safer page fetching. |
| **Housekeeping**<br>*PL-01, PL-02, PL-09, PL-12* | The code checker fails on build output, documentation has drifted, retired code and unused tables remain, and one end-to-end test can pass without testing anything. | Exclude build folders from the checker; fix documentation drift; remove retired code, with table changes in an agreed window; make the full-pipeline test approve, collect and measure. | Clean checks and trustworthy documentation and tests. |

## How the work is sequenced

| Phase | Goal | What it means for users |
|---|---|---|
| **R0: Hygiene and calibration** | Make today's numbers trustworthy and measure how noisy they are | Reports re-analyzed; noise floor published; no change to the questions |
| **R1: Accuracy and labeling** | Fix how answers are read and counted; label every question; make collection durable | One "Update results" per run; clearer, better-labeled figures |
| **R2: Question set v2** | Ship every question-set and competitor-set change together | Each project's trend breaks once, onto a new, better baseline |
| **R3: Engines, places and languages** | Add Gemini or Google surfaces, language sets and slower diagnostics as separate baselines | New measurements alongside, never mixed into, the English ChatGPT results |

> **Owner decisions the plan depends on.** The Discovery question count; repeat answers versus more questions; run cadence and answer budget; where each business competes (district, city or country); evidence-based ownership claims; Gemini or Google surfaces; Arabic or Hindi pilots; and an agreed window for database changes.
>
> **What stays unchanged.** The four-stage journey, honest "x of n" reporting with no composite scores, no collection vendor named in the product, no paid spend without approval, and no hard-coded brands or competitors.
